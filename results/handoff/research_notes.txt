SUMMARY: Both targets have open weights, but neither has a small sibling whose states line up with it closely enough for a direct handoff. Kimi K3 (2.78T total / 104.2B active, 93 layers) interleaves 69 KDA layers with 24 NoPE Gated MLA layers. It also adds Attention Residuals, so each layer reads a softmax mix of 9 block representations instead of one residual stream. A handoff would have to supply 69 KDA recurrent states (96 heads x 128 x 128 each) plus those block representations. The only small model with the same attention types is Kimi Linear 48B-A3B (3B active, about 35x fewer active params). It shares the exact tiktoken BPE file but has no AttnRes, uses a different KDA decay and gate parameterization, and maps different chat special tokens to IDs 163586-163588. GLM-5.3 (753B / ~40B active, 78 layers) has DSA on MLA (kv_lora 512, nope 192, rope 64, v 256, indexer 32x128, top-k 2048, indexer computed every 4th layer). GLM-4.7-Flash (30B-A3B) shares its byte-identical tokenizer.json and its MLA per-head dims, but it has 20 heads instead of 64 and no DSA indexer. GLM-5.3-Flash (320B-A18B) also shares the tokenizer, but its attention is KDA plus NoPE sparse MLA with mHC, so its states are not the same type as GLM-5.3's. On the serving side, the evidence says prefill handoff targets a small cost for coding. Moonshot reports a typical 1M-context coding request carries a 400K-token cached prefix and only a 4K-token prefill increment. SemiAnalysis AgentX coding traces have a median of 142,016 input and 444 output tokens per request with a 95%+ KV hit rate. Cached input is billed at one tenth of uncached input ($0.30 vs $3.00 per M for K3), and output costs $15 per M. Provider latency for reasoning modes is dominated by thinking tokens (time to first answer token 4 to 8 s at 10K input). Speculative drafters that read K3 hidden states already accept 4.2 to 5.5 tokens per step on code. So for coding agents, a handoff saves at most the uncached per-turn increment. It would also have to keep KDA checkpoints and MLA/indexer caches in step with the cached prefix. One-pass candidate decisions (tool choice, option letters) cut decode steps, and speculative decoding already captures part of that gain. Combined with Heo et al.'s far-pair collapse and the user's earlier frozen-receiver failures, the likely payoff on these two targets is small. The most plausible test pair is GLM-4.7-Flash to GLM-5.3 (MLA latents only), not any Kimi pair.

- [k3-kda-state] (verified) KDA keeps one recurrent state S_t in R^{dk x dv} per head, updated as S_t = (I - beta_t k_t k_t^T) Diag(alpha_t) S_{t-1} + beta_t k_t v_t^T, with output S_t^T q_t. Per-token inputs: q and k are L2Norm(Swish(ShortConv(W x))), v is Swish(ShortConv(W x)), beta is Sigmoid(W x), and decay logits z come from a low-rank projection plus bias. K3 bounds the log-decay as g = g_min * Sigmoid(e^A z) with g_min = -5 and adds a full-rank sigmoid output gate after a head-wise RMSNorm. Derived from the config: 69 layers x 96 heads x 128 x 128 is about 108.5M state values per sequence (about 217 MB at BF16; the serving dtype is not stated). | S in R^{128x128} per head; g_min=-5; alpha > e^-5 ~ 6.7e-3; derived 108,527,616 state values per sequence | https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3/main/k3_tech_report.pdf
- [k3-nope-attnres] (corrected) Kimi K3 uses no positional encoding anywhere (NoPE in all MLA layers); position comes only from KDA recurrence and decay. Block Attention Residuals replace the plain residual. Each layer attends with a learned pseudo-query over the embedding and the preceding block representations (softmax over RMSNorm keys). K3 has 8 blocks of 12 layers, 9 sources counting the embedding. A latent handoff therefore has no single residual stream to inject into. It would need to supply the block representations as well as the KDA states and MLA latents. | 8 blocks x 12 layers, 9 block sources incl. embedding | https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3/main/k3_tech_report.pdf
    VERIFY: K3 applies NoPE to all MLA layers. Position is encoded only implicitly, through KDA's recurrent gating and decay. Block Attention Residuals use a learned per-layer pseudo-query and a softmax kernel exp(q^T RMSNorm(k)) over the block representations b_0 (embedding), ..., b_{n-1}, plus the current block's partial sum b_n^{i-1} for layers after the first in a block. The final output layer aggregates all block representations. K3's 93 layers are split into 8 blocks of 12-layer size; the final block is partial (9 layers). Counting the embedding gives 9 total blocks. A handoff at a mid-network layer would therefore need the prior block representations and the current block's partial sum, as well as the KDA states and MLA latents. :: Report Sec 2.1.2 says NoPE in all MLA layers. Sec 3.4 says position comes implicitly through KDA gating and decay. Sec 2.2 (Eq. 8-10) says: 'we partition its layers into 8 blocks with 12-layer size, giving a partial final block and 9 total blocks when counting the embedding layer'. Eq. 10 includes the intra-block partial sum b_n^{i-1}. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3/main/k3_t

    VERIFY: The report says: 'At 1M context, a typical coding input carries a prefix of 400K tokens but requires a prefill increment of only 4K tokens.' Production traffic mixes requests under 2K tokens with requests up to 1M. Prefix hashing uses fine hash blocks (e.g. 512 tokens) inside 6144-token physical blocks (12 hash blocks), and KDA forces the shared block size to 1024-6144 tokens. KDA checkpoints are saved only at a sparse subset of hash endpoints. They are retained mostly at conversation-turn boundaries, because intermediate checkpoints are recycled as the request advances. VERIFIER NOTES: I checked all 25 claims against primary sources: HF configs and API, the K3 tech report PDF (read locally after WebFetch saved it), vLLM recipes, docs.z.ai and the arXiv GLM-5 report. 15 are verified and 10 are corrected. None are refuted.

Corrections that matter for prefill handoff:
1. K3 prefix cache: KDA checkpoints are kept only at a sparse subset of hash boundaries, mostly conversation-turn boundaries. A handoff can only resume from those points.
2. K3 Block AttnRes: also needs the current block's partial sum. The final block is partial, since 93 layers = 7x12 + 9.
3. GLM-4.7-Flash to GLM-5.3: the KV mapping also has to deal with different RoPE bases (1e6 vs 8e6) and with GLM-5.3's DSA indexer key cache, which the small model lacks.
4. GLM-5.3: the vLLM recipe's default hardware is 8x B300, not H200. 744B/40B is GLM-5's headline; GLM-5.3 itself shows 753B on HF and no active count.
5. GLM-5.3-Flash: the 3.01x attention and 4.44x KV figures are in Z.ai's own docs. AgentX's 95%+ hit rate comes from the SemiAnalysis newsletter, which also gives a lower median ISL (88k) than the methodology page (142,016).

Two minor inconsistencies:
- The GLM-5 report says 80 layers, while the GLM-5 and GLM-5.3 configs say 78.
- The GLM-5.3-Flash HF API single-model createdAt read as 2026-09-07, but the org listing shows 2026-08-25.

######## transfer
SUMMARY: I found no published method that reads input with a small model, hands the states to a large model, skips the large model's prefill, and beats the small model alone on reasoning or coding. The closest cases are near pairs from the same family on easy tasks. In Heo et al., even the best pair (Qwen3 14B to 32B) scores below the 14B source alone on MMLU (78.09 vs 78.86) and GSM8K (90.98 vs 95.00). The far pairs collapse; Llama 8B to 70B gets 14.78 on GSM8K against 56.10 for the 8B alone. KV Prediction from Apple is the only small-to-large state transfer with a coding number. On HumanEval pass@1 it gets 10.12, between the 450M auxiliary alone (8.85) and the 1.1B base (15.79), and that is with the auxiliary and predictor trained jointly. The methods that do beat a single model all keep the receiver's own prefill, so they add compute instead of saving it: C2C, activation communication, and LatentMAS. LatentMAS also uses the same model on both sides. A mismatched-cache audit found that C2C passed no detectable sender-private content. The speed gains in this literature grow with input length: HeteroFold is 3.75x faster than native prefill at 4K tokens and 10.74x at 32K, and Heo et al. report 25x at 32K. On short prompts that are bound by weight bandwidth, which is what you measured on Gemma 4 26B-A4B, the big model's prefill is a small slice of total latency, so handoff has little to save. Architecture coverage does not match either target. Every KV-transfer paper tests dense GQA/MHA models. Heo et al. exclude hybrid, sliding-window and SSM models. LatentPort is the only work on hybrid linear attention (Qwen3.5 4B to 9B, with the Gated DeltaNet state copied directly because its shape matches), and it reports only teacher-forced loss and says no production latency saving follows. Nothing tests MLA, DeepSeek Sparse Attention, Kimi Delta Attention, or a MoE receiver for KV transfer. The one MoE receiver is xRAG on Mixtral, which uses a single soft token. No cross-model method reports SWE-bench or LiveCodeBench. For Kimi K3 or GLM-5.3 in coding and general use, this evidence points away from prefill handoff for short prompts and one-pass decisions. On long repo-scale inputs the speed case is real. But quality drops with length, and you would need a new mapper for MLA/DSA or KDA plus a validation from scratch. Token selection by a small model (SpecPrefill) is a lower-risk TTFT lever for long context, but its authors say it is weak on short, dense prompts such as code.

SUMMARY: The sweep found that neither target gets much prefill relief at the short input lengths where a one-pass decision would run. GLM-5.3 (same base as GLM-5.2: glm_moe_dsa, 78 layers, index_topk 2048) uses DSA, which only sparsifies attention once the context is longer than 2048 tokens. DeepSeek runs short prefills in a dense "masked MHA" mode. Kimi K3 (69 KDA + 24 MLA layers) gets its KDA speedups mainly at long context: the 48B Kimi Linear prototype prefills 2.3x faster than full MLA at 128K and 2.9x faster at 1M. At a few hundred tokens, prefill on both models is dominated by MoE expert-weight traffic, and dropping input tokens barely helps. Your own Gemma numbers (8.1 ms for 1 token vs 10.9 ms for 8) show the same flat regime. Where prefill does cost something (long first reads of repos or documents), there are methods that keep the big model computing its own KV, which avoids the frozen-receiver failure your latent handoff hit. Small-model token selection (SpecPrefill and its cross-family variant) has been tested on MLA MoE targets: DeepSeek-V3 went from 46 s to about 2.5 s TTFT at 128K compressed to 16K, and DeepSeek-V3.1 kept 95.9% of its Code Debug score at a 20-30% keep rate. For code, the code-aware compressor LongCodeZip keeps completion accuracy at 4.3-5.6x compression, while generic LLMLingua-2 roughly halves exact match. Coding agents run about 100:1 input to output, but most of that input is prefix-cached, and vLLM now supports block-granular KDA state caching (98.5% hit rate on Kimi Linear). So the uncached part of each agent turn is small. On the decode side, the two serving numbers come from different setups, so compare them with care. With a DSpark draft model, Kimi K3 goes from 118 to 370 tok/s per user at batch 1 on vLLM, about 4.7-5.0 accepted tokens on code; K3 ships with no built-in MTP head (num_nextn_predict_layers 0). GLM-5.2/5.3 has an MTP head that often accepts 5+ tokens, and SGLang reports more than 500 TPS at batch 1 on 8xB300. Splitwise found that a 1500-token prompt costs about the same time as 6 output tokens. Average completions on OpenRouter are around 400 tokens. So for general chat and for code generation, decode dominates wall-clock time, and speculative decoding is the bigger lever. A one-pass decision only removes decode for steps whose answer is one item from a fixed set (tool choice, routing, multiple choice). It cannot write code. GLM-5.3-Flash is a different architecture from GLM-5.3 (glm5_next, 45 layers, hidden 4096, KDA plus DSA hybrid), so it is not a matched-KV source for Heo-style mapping. It does share GLM-5.3's 154,880-token vocabulary, which makes it a plain speculator for SpecPrefill-style selection or drafting.

######## economics
SUMMARY: On cached agent loops, prefill handoff has little left to save. One-pass readouts go after the part that costs time, which is decode, but they save little money, and Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 both always think before answering. Production coding-agent traces show inputs outnumbering outputs by roughly 300:1 to 1,100:1: TraceLab (Claude Code and Codex) and GitHub Copilot's June 2026 production traces. 90 to 96% of input tokens hit the prefix cache. A median step re-reads a 116K to 126K token cached prefix, appends only 857 to 886 new tokens and decodes 184 to 252 tokens. Under 0.1x cache-read pricing, re-reading the prefix is 59.5% of API cost, appended tokens 29.2% and output 11.2%. With caching, decode takes 91 to 98.6% of LLM time and prefill 1.4 to 9%. LLM generation is about 41% of request wall time; tool execution is the other 59.8%. General chat looks different: the Mooncake Kimi trace averages 7,955 input to 194 output tokens with about 50% prefix reuse, and OpenRouter-wide averages are over 6K prompt to about 400 completion tokens. So in agentic coding on K3 ($3.00 miss / $0.30 hit / $15 output) or GLM-5.3 ($1.40 / $0.26 / $4.40), handing the appended-token prefill to a small model saves about 4 to 7% of per-step API cost and a few tens of ms. It saves more only at cache-cold points: new user turns (84.4% hit), model switches (8%), compaction, and chat over fresh long documents. A one-pass decision removes the decode, which on 8x B300 is about 10.5 ms per output token. That turns a 184-token step of about 2 to 4 s into one forward. It still pays for reading the cached prefix, so it saves only 2.5 to 7% of dollars. Prior art fits routing (RouteLLM's causal classifier is a softmax over label tokens), tool selection (Octopus v2 functional tokens), reranking (Qwen3-Reranker, FIRST) and verifiers (R2E-Gym P(YES), GenRM). In all of these the readout was trained for the job. Untrained first-token readouts disagree with text answers over 60% of the time. Both target models always reason (reasoning_effort low/high/max, default max), so a no-thinking readout at the answer position is off-distribution. Tool calls also need arguments, so a readout can only pick the tool name. For K3 specifically, 69 of 93 layers are KDA layers with a fixed-size recurrent state. A per-head KV mapping like Heo et al.'s would only cover the 24 MLA layers. vLLM caches KDA state only at intervals and prompt boundaries.

VERIFIER NOTES: I checked all 25 claims: 18 verified, 7 corrected, none refuted, none left unverifiable.

Corrections:
- C7: the paper has no TTFT figure of "~25% of generation". It does give per-step means (LLM 11.5 s, tool 16.8 s) next to the medians.
- C11: the article does not mention Mooncake. The >90% figure is a Moonshot claim the article itself calls unverified.
- C19: the 85/45/35% numbers come from the LMSYS blog, not the arXiv paper.
- C22: GenRM's 73% to 93.4% is problems solved on Gemini 1.0 Pro solutions using a Gemma2-9B CoT verifier with 32-vote majority.
- C23: "over 60%" is the worst-case mismatch for some models, not a typical rate.

Caveats that matter for the prefill-handoff question:
(a) Copilot (C3) token counts exclude reasoning tokens. TraceLab outputs include them.
(b) Agentic coding has 95.7% prefix-cache hits (TraceLab), and in Yuan et al. prefill is only 1.4-9.0% of LLM time. Handing off the uncached prefill attacks a small slice: about 6.6% of step cost on K3 and about 3.9% on GLM-5.3. The larger share of latency and cost is decode and cached-prefix reads.
(c) Both K3 and GLM-5.3 have reasoning always on and no off switch (C12, C13). A one-pass readout runs the models outside their served mode, and the 'My Answer is C' paper (C23) shows untrained first-token readouts are unreliable.
(d) The GLM-5.3 config shows DSA indexer layers shared 3 of every 4 (index_topk_freq 4), a detail not in the original claim.
(e) The vLLM K3 blog does not give a GPU count for its 'B300 node' or say whether DSpark was on in the DCP8 run.

Not found in a primary source: GLM-5.3 active parameters; a first-party Moonshot source for the >90% coding cache-hit claim; any direct prefill tokens/s figure for K3.
